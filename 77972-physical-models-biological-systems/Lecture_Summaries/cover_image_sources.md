# Cover image sources

The cover combines a generated cell illustration, Korinna's branching bacterial colony photograph, and the NOAA phytoplankton photograph. They illustrate cellular mechanisms, microbial populations, and marine microorganisms; they do not depict the same experiment or a shared spatial scale.

## Generated cell illustration

- File: `cover_biological_systems.png`, 1024 × 1536 pixels.
- Created with the built-in image generation tool on 2026-10-15.
- Conceptual artwork, not a microscopy image or a quantitatively accurate diagram.
- Full prompt: `cover_biological_systems.prompt.md`.
- Displayed at 55 mm tall, approximately 709 pixels per inch.

## Fluorescent microbial colony alternative (not used on the final cover)

- File: `cover_colony_source.png`, 4316 × 3604 pixels.
- Original: Isaac Nuñez et al. (2017), *Low cost and open source multi-fluorescence imaging system for teaching and research in biology and bioengineering*, PLOS ONE 12(11): e0187163, Figure 3E.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0187163.g003
- Article: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0187163
- License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
- Download: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/figure/image?download=&id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0187163.g003&size=original
- The original TIFF was converted losslessly to PNG for pdfLaTeX. The full figure is preserved as an alternative. A preview selected the six-day green/orange colony in the upper-right part of panel E, retaining the time label and 500 μm scale bar. No colors or experimental content were changed.
- The study uses fluorescently labelled E. coli populations to observe colony sectoring during growth. This connects the cover to population dynamics, biological variability, and quantitative observation in the syllabus; the picture alone does not establish a particular evolutionary mechanism.
